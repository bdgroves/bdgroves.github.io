30 Themes · 30 Maps · One November

Thirty Maps in Thirty Days

Lakewood, WA · December 2026 Brooks Groves
Cartography GIS Python Open Data Groveland Pacific Northwest

Every November, mapmakers all over the world take on the #30DayMapChallenge: thirty themes, one for each day, and a map for every one of them. This year I did all thirty. Each one went out to X and Bluesky on its day, and now that the month is over, here they all are in one place.

The themes sent me all over the place, from a 1772 chart of New Zealand to the census blocks of Nevada where nobody lives. But the maps kept finding their way home. Seven of them are about Groveland, the Tuolumne and the country I grew up in, and four are about Mount Rainier.

A few of them tried something new. Day 7 was made live in QGIS against a ten-minute clock and finished at 5:09. Day 10 is a FORTRAN program, written from a single one-sentence prompt, that prints Big Creek's watershed the way a 1960s line printer would. Day 27 counts all 3.8 million buildings in Washington with one DuckDB query. And Day 30 is drawn by hand, in bar ink on a cocktail napkin.

Most of the maps are Python (25 of the 30), built and rendered on GitHub Actions, with Mapbox basemaps (this year's sponsor) where streets and water help tell the story. Every map below links to its own page with the data sources and the stories behind it.

Week one: points to the 10-minute map

Day 1 · Points
1,190 caches
Every geocache I've found as Hipparchus, one dot each, colored by year. Twenty-odd years of where curiosity has taken me.
Day 2 · Lines
The Seawall
The path around Stanley Park in Vancouver: 8.8 km of Seawall between the forest and the sea, 10 km all the way round, on a Mapbox Outdoors basemap, with the landmarks along the way.
Day 3 · Polygons
Every fire around Groveland
Every recorded fire perimeter in Tuolumne County since the early 1900s, oldest faint, newest bright, with the Rim Fire (2013) wrapped around Groveland.
Day 4 · Clusters
Rainier's swarms
Earthquakes within 30 km of Mount Rainier since 2000, grouped into swarms by space and time. The July 2025 swarm, the largest ever recorded there, stands out.
Day 5 · Sight
Where you can see Rainier from
A viewshed from the summit of Mount Rainier over 90 m terrain, with Earth's curvature and refraction, showing every place in Washington with a line of sight to the mountain.
Day 6 · Vintage
Tasman to Cook
James Cook's 1772 chart of New Zealand, lined up with today's coast, with the stretches Abel Tasman saw in 1642–43 in gold. A map version of my Cartographic Perspectives article.
Day 7 · 10 minute map
Every fire since 1950, Los Angeles
Made in QGIS against the clock, finished at 5:09: every CAL FIRE perimeter around Los Angeles since 1950, stacked and colored by year, with the Palisades and Eaton fires of January 2025 outlined in white.

Week two: Utopia to Borges

Day 8 · Utopia
Paradise, Eden, Utopia
Every place in the United States named Paradise, Eden, Utopia, Arcadia or Shangri-La, from Paradise on Rainier to Utopia, Texas.
Day 9 · Urban-rural
Tacoma to Paradise
One line from the Port of Tacoma to Paradise on Rainier, and what changes along it: people, buildings, trees and elevation.
Day 10 · Prompting only
A map made only by asking
Big Creek's watershed above Groveland as a 1960s line-printer map: a FORTRAN program, written entirely from a one-sentence prompt, prints the basin in eight overprinted tones on green-bar paper, the way SYMAP did. The prompts are posted with it.
Day 11 · Sound
Where the tsunami sirens reach
Washington's tsunami sirens, each with the one-mile circle it's designed to be heard in, over the tsunami hazard zones they're there to empty. (Pierce County's lahar sirens were the first idea, but their locations aren't published.)
Day 12 · Power
The Tuolumne powers San Francisco
Hetch Hetchy's dams, the powerhouse at Moccasin just below Groveland, and the lines that carry the Tuolumne's power to San Francisco.
Day 13 · Interactions
Follow a raindrop
Click anywhere in the lower 48 and watch where the water goes, river by river, to the sea or a lake with no outlet, using the USGS's navigation service. From Groveland, it is 279 km to the Delta.
Day 14 · Borgesian map
1:1
Borges's map the size of the empire, made literal. Big Creek's watershed at full scale as a page you can scroll for miles, with an odometer showing how far you've gone.

Week three: inside Kīlauea to OpenStreetMap

Day 15 · Inside out
Inside Kīlauea
The earthquakes beneath Kīlauea's summit in cross-section, sketching the magma plumbing that the surface hides.
Day 16 · Collaborative map
Washington's favorite outdoors, by vote
Made by the people who voted: every outdoor winner of KING 5 Evening's Best Northwest Escapes viewer poll I could find, bigger the more it won. Deception Pass leads.
Day 17 · Light & dark
Great Basin's dark sky
Night lights across Nevada and Utah, with Great Basin National Park's dark sky set against the glow of Las Vegas and Salt Lake City.
Day 18 · NULL
Nobody lives here
Nevada's 2020 census blocks with a population of zero. Most of the state is null.
Day 19 · Smell
What the Yakima Valley smells like
Washington's hop fields, and its hop acreage sorted by the first smell HopLove lists for each variety: tropical fruit, citrus, resin, spice, berry and more.
Day 20 · Hexagons
Kangaroo rats in hexagons
Every Dipodomys observation across the Southwest and Great Basin, binned into H3 hexagons. A return to my 2023 Day 1.
Day 21 · OpenStreetMap
Groveland, by volunteers
Everything OpenStreetMap knows about Groveland and Big Oak Flat, the towns where I grew up, colored by the year a volunteer last touched it, and how many people drew it.

The last stretch: projections to pen and paper

Day 22 · Projections
The world from Groveland
An azimuthal equidistant map centered on Groveland, where I grew up, with straight lines (true distances) to everywhere I've been since, from Namibia to Guadeloupe.
Day 23 · Taste
Every brewery I've checked into
My Untappd check-ins, mapped by brewery, sized by how many beers.
Day 24 · Network
Every stream that reaches Modesto
The whole Tuolumne river network above Modesto, by stream order, with the SIERRA-FLOW gages on it.
Day 25 · Is this a map?
The Beast Quake
January 8, 2011: the Seahawks' playoff win over the Saints on the seismometer across the street from the stadium, ten minutes a line, with Marshawn Lynch's 67-yard touchdown run shaking the ground at 4:42 PM. A map of a game, or of a place?
Day 26 · Water
Lake Tahoe, clear and deep
Tahoe's floor, 497 m at the deepest, and beside it the lake's clarity on the same downward axis: how far a Secchi disk could be seen each year since 1968, from the record my SECCHI project keeps.
Day 27 · New tool
Every building in Washington
All 3.8 million building footprints in Washington from Overture Maps, counted into half-kilometer cells by one DuckDB query run straight against Overture's files on S3. New to me.
Day 28 · Feeling
Did you feel it? Nisqually, 2001
The M6.8 Nisqually earthquake of February 28, 2001, as the people who felt it reported it, from USGS "Did You Feel It?".
Day 29 · Raster
When Rainier's snow melted
Every 30 m patch of Mount Rainier colored by the day it lost its snow in 2026, from Sentinel-2's scene classification, with the glaciers that never melted in white and Paradise's SNOTEL record from my snowpack tracker as ground truth.
Day 30 · Pen & paper
If you hit the third cattle guard
The hot-spring napkin from my memoir, redrawn by hand in bar ink on a cocktail napkin: the springs from Bridgeport to Long Valley and the roads between them, with directions counted in cattle guards, not miles. Drawn from memory, like the original.

Behind the maps

All of the code, data notes and finished maps are on GitHub in bdgroves/30DayMapChallenge, and the 2026 gallery has every map at full size with share buttons. My 2023 and 2024 maps are there too. Day 20's kangaroo rats are a return to my 2023 Day 1.

Thanks to everyone who liked, shared or replied along the way, and to the organizers who put the challenge together every year. Which one was your favorite? Let me know.
