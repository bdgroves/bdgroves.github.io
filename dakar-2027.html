The Route

The Dakar goes back to Africa. On 25 October ASO and the Saudi Motorsport Company called off the Saudi edition over the instability in the Middle East, and five days later ASO published the replacement: a start in Portugal’s Algarve, a ferry across the Mediterranean, and twelve stages across Morocco to Marrakech. It is the first Dakar in Africa since 2007, and the last two African editions started in Portugal too, from Lisbon. Saudi Arabia gets the rally back for its 50th edition in 2028.

The prologue runs at the Algarve stadium between Loulé and Faro on 9 January. Stage 1 heads east to the Strait of Gibraltar and an overnight ferry to Nador, and a 590 km transfer reaches Bouârfa, near the Algerian border, for two loop stages. Stage 4 crosses the Merzouga dunes to Zagora in the Draa valley, and stage 6 runs west to Agadir and the rest day on the Atlantic.

The second week goes south. Stage 7 has the longest special of the rally, 500 km from Agadir to Smara. From Smara the marathon goes out to a refuge bivouac with no assistance and comes back the next day, and a loop follows. Stage 11 is the longest day, 1,035 km north to Marrakech, and a 22 km special there finishes it on 23 January. That’s 4,341 km of specials in 7,824 km, against 5,320 km of specials in the Saudi plan; most specials run between 350 and 500 km.

Stages are numbered and drawn straight from town to town; rings mark loop stages that start and finish in the same town, and the dashed line is the untimed transfer. Each stage’s special and liaisons share one line, because ASO publishes the distances but not the tracks, and crews get each day’s roadbook the night before. The refuge bivouac and the ferry port haven’t been named, so those two points (hollow) are read off ASO’s route map. Imagery © Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics.

Stages to Watch
Stage 4 · Thursday 14 January
Bouârfa to Zagora, through Merzouga
The first big day of sand: 490 km of special that crosses the Merzouga dunes, the Erg Chebbi, on the way to the Draa valley. It’s the first real test of the trucks.
Stage 7 · Monday 18 January
500 kilometres, straight after the rest day
The longest special of the rally runs south from Agadir to Smara, the morning after the rest day. Whatever the mechanics rebuilt in Agadir gets tested here.
Stages 8–9 · 19–20 January
The refuge: no assistance, no mechanics
The rally’s one marathon. Stage 8 ends at a refuge bivouac away from the teams, and whatever breaks, the crew fixes with what’s on the vehicle before setting off again at dawn. Help is waiting back in Smara at the end of stage 9, 745 km of special later.
Stage 11 · Friday 22 January
1,035 kilometres to Marrakech
The longest day of the rally: a 455 km special and 580 km of road from Smara north to Marrakech, the day before the finish. The last special is only 22 km, but last January the bike title came down to two seconds, so even that one counts.
A word about the trucks

The T5 class is the reason a lot of people watch this. Eight tonnes of truck, around a thousand horsepower, three crew inside — driver, co-driver, and a mechanic who spends the day being thrown around a cab with a toolkit.

They are not built to fly and they do it anyway, over dunes, at speed, landing on suspension that has to survive another two weeks. They run the same route as the bikes and the cars, in the same time, and when one of them gets stuck it takes a second truck to pull it out.

Since 1979

Thierry Sabine got lost in the Libyan desert on a motorbike in 1977, decided the place was worth showing other people, and ran the first Paris–Dakar two years later.

It ran to Senegal until 2007, moved to South America in 2009 after the Dakar route was cancelled over security, and has been in Saudi Arabia since 2020. For 2027 it moves again, to Portugal and Morocco, before going back to Saudi Arabia for the 50th edition in 2028.
