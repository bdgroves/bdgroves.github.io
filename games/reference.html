🎲 Referee's Screen

Every rule both games actually run on, laid out in panels. Written down partly so the games stay consistent as they grow, and partly because a screen was always the accessory that made these things playable at a table.

All three games use original mechanics — nothing here is reproduced from any published rulebook.
Kellath's Drift rolls percentile and wants to roll low. Offline Protocol and Adjuster's Report roll an ascending die ladder and want to roll high.
All three games have synthesized dice audio — there's a sound toggle in each game's bottom bar.
This page prints cleanly if you want it on paper.

🚀 Kellath's Drift — percentile
Play →

The Core Roll

Roll 2d10 — one die is tens, one is ones.
A read of 00 counts as 100.

Success if the roll is ≤ your target.

Lower is better. The target number is built from an ability score, then adjusted by specialization and gear.

Building a Target Number

Target = ½ ability score
  + 15 if your PSA covers it
  + 10 if relevant gear in hand
  ± run drift (−3 to +3)
  (minimum 5)

A few checks use the full ability score instead of half — those are the ones the story frames as brute-force rather than finesse.

The Five Facts

Badge logA fourth, stripped cycle
Suit recordSigned but not serviced
Medical timelineDeath 19 min before the alarm
Unsent messageANNEX — THIRD REQUEST
Floor scuffsTwo sets, one not his size

Each is established once or not at all. A failed check burns the hour and closes that line for the contract.

The Clock

9 hours to the return shuttle.
Every evidence check: −1
Every press or confrontation: −1

There are five facts and three suspects and not quite enough hours to do all of it carefully. That shortfall is the game.

The Contradiction

Each suspect has exactly one document that breaks their account — but the option to produce it only means something against the one who is lying.

Holtbroken by the badge log
Marekbroken by the suit record
Vannbroken by the medical timeline

Hold it up to an innocent suspect and it still fits — that's the trap. They read it, hand it back, and tell you they flagged it themselves three days ago.

Reading the Reaction

The only reliable tell in the game is what happens after you put the document on the table.

Guilty: doesn't say it's wrong.
  "That does not mean what you are going to write down that it means."

Innocent: reads it, hands it back, explains it.

Innocent suspects also give you the unflattering truth when pressed. People constructing a story rarely construct one that makes them look that bad.

Because the guilty party is drawn fresh, you cannot solve this once and replay from memory — the evidence means something different every contract.

The Actual Choice

The policy pays eleven million on misadventure and nothing if a covered party did it. The brief says confirm and close.

So there are two questions in this game and only one of them is a puzzle. The other is what you write down once you already know.
