## Where the Trail Runs Out (RIDGELINE)

Save as: `blog/img/ridgeline-post/og.jpg`

```
Create a wide, cinematic website hero image for my project.

PROJECT TITLE: "RIDGELINE"

SOURCE MATERIAL:
If a README, webpage, or article is attached, read it before designing the image. Identify the project's central scientific story, geographic setting, data pipeline, and most visually meaningful process.

PROJECT SUMMARY:
RIDGELINE maps every mountain, technical and water rescue call that Phoenix, Scottsdale and Boulder fire departments publish, plus national-park search-and-rescue incidents, and asks whether Phoenix's summer trail heat closures worked.

KEY VISUAL IDEAS:
- Desert mountain rescue at the edge of a city: steep rocky trails climbing out of neighbourhood trailheads in summer heat
- Phoenix at dusk, with Camelback Mountain and Piestewa Peak rising straight out of the city grid and city lights below
- Rescue locations as small glowing points clustered along the trails and trailheads, with faint contour lines and a heat shimmer over the slopes
- A subtle hint of a second mountain town's sandstone ridgeline far in the distance (Boulder's Flatirons), as a quiet echo, not a second scene
- A trail that fades out where the rescues cluster: 'where the trail runs out'

DESIRED HOUSE STYLE:
Create a sophisticated scientific-editorial illustration that blends cinematic landscape photography, realistic natural textures, geospatial visualization, and restrained data art. It should feel technically credible and atmospheric—appropriate for a GeoAI, environmental science, natural-history, or research project.

Use a panoramic 16:9 composition designed for a website hero and social sharing. Build the scene around the actual process the project studies rather than showing a generic dashboard screenshot. Integrate maps, grids, contours, sensor points, waveforms, scientific traces, or other data elements naturally into the landscape. They should feel embedded in the world, not pasted on as a user interface.

Keep the composition visually rich but readable. Use strong depth, a clear visual path, realistic terrain or environmental detail, and one memorable scientific idea. Leave some breathing room around the title.

TITLE:
Add only the exact title "RIDGELINE" in the upper-left. Use large, restrained uppercase lettering with generous tracking. Select a typography treatment appropriate to the project—geometric scientific, technical monospaced, engraved field-guide, or refined editorial. Add one short, thin accent rule beneath it.

COLOR AND MOOD:
Use deep navy, charcoal, slate, and natural earth tones as the foundation, with restrained teal, cyan, amber, copper, or another project-specific accent. The image should feel mysterious, intelligent, exploratory, and grounded in real science—not apocalyptic or fantastical.

IMPORTANT CONSTRAINTS:
- Spell the title exactly.
- Add no other words, labels, numbers, logos, or watermarks.
- Do not create a dashboard, screenshot, infographic panel, or fake chart interface.
- Avoid generic stock-photo composition.
- Avoid neon cyberpunk styling.
- Avoid sensational disaster imagery unless it is genuinely central to the project.
- Keep scientific subjects plausible rather than cartoonish.
- Do not invent major factual elements that are absent from the supplied source.
- Make the result visually related to a polished series of environmental and geoscience project heroes, while giving this project its own distinctive identity.
- Do not show injured people, helicopters mid-rescue or emergency drama; keep it calm and analytical.
- Generate the finished image directly without asking follow-up questions unless a truly essential detail is missing.
```
