## KELLATH'S DRIFT

Save as: `images/features/game-kellaths-drift-2.jpg` (the second round; the serif title matches the others)

```
Create a wide, cinematic website hero image for my project.

PROJECT TITLE: "KELLATH'S DRIFT"

SOURCE MATERIAL:
Read the attached README, webpage, or article before designing the image. Identify the project's central scientific story, geographic setting, data pipeline, and most visually meaningful process.

PROJECT SUMMARY:
A short percentile-dice sci-fi adventure in the spirit of the classic TSR tabletop RPGs: build a scout, crash-land on a frontier moon, and reach the relay tower before life support runs out. This is a small browser game on my site, so treat the picture as a game's key art: one dramatic moment from the story, cinematic and grounded, painted rather than cartoonish, with no game interface, buttons or dice on screen.

KEY VISUAL IDEAS:
- A lone scout in a worn environment suit trudging across a frontier moon toward a distant relay tower, oxygen frost on the visor
- An alien moon at dusk: rust-coloured plains, jagged ridges, a huge ringed gas giant filling half the sky
- The crashed scout ship's trail cutting a long scar across the regolith; faint beacon pulses from the tower
- A retro 1980s science-fiction paperback-cover feel, a nod to Star Frontiers
- The relay tower's red light blinking, still a long way off

DESIRED HOUSE STYLE:
Create a sophisticated scientific-editorial illustration that blends cinematic landscape photography, realistic natural textures, geospatial visualization, and restrained data art. It should feel technically credible and atmospheric—appropriate for a GeoAI, environmental science, natural-history, or research project.

Use a panoramic 16:9 composition designed for a website hero and social sharing. Build the scene around the actual process the project studies rather than showing a generic dashboard screenshot. Integrate maps, grids, contours, sensor points, waveforms, scientific traces, or other data elements naturally into the landscape. They should feel embedded in the world, not pasted on as a user interface.

Keep the composition visually rich but readable. Use strong depth, a clear visual path, realistic terrain or environmental detail, and one memorable scientific idea. Leave some breathing room around the title.

TITLE:
Add only the exact title "KELLATH'S DRIFT" in the upper-left. Use large, restrained uppercase lettering with generous tracking. Select a typography treatment appropriate to the project—geometric scientific, technical monospaced, engraved field-guide, or refined editorial. Add one short, thin accent rule beneath it.

COLOR AND MOOD:
Use deep navy, charcoal, slate, and natural earth tones as the foundation, with restrained teal, cyan, amber, copper, or another project-specific accent. The image should feel mysterious, intelligent, exploratory, and grounded in real science—not apocalyptic or fantastical.

IMPORTANT CONSTRAINTS:
- Spell the title exactly.
- Add no other words, labels, numbers, logos, or watermarks.
- Do not create a dashboard, screenshot, infographic panel, or fake chart interface.
- Avoid generic stock-photo composition.
- Avoid neon cyberpunk styling.
- Avoid sensational disaster imagery unless it is genuinely central to the project.
- Keep scientific subjects plausible rather than cartoonish.
- Do not invent major factual elements that are absent from the supplied source.
- Make the result visually related to a polished series of environmental and geoscience project heroes, while giving this project its own distinctive identity.
- Generate the finished image directly without asking follow-up questions unless a truly essential detail is missing.
```

- A well-worn paper road atlas open across the back seat, its map of the United States with about a third of the states shaded in warm gold, as if filled in by hand along the way

PROJECT TITLE: "FLAG ATLAS"

SOURCE MATERIAL:
Read the attached README, webpage, or article before designing the image. Identify the project's central story, geographic setting, data, and most visually meaningful moment.

PROJECT SUMMARY:
A flag guessing game that goes far past countries: more than 15,000 flags of countries, states, provinces, cities, counties and towns around the world. Name each flag, or pin it on a map with no names on it, then learn where it flies and the story behind its design, with a new Flag of the Day for everyone. A robot gathers the flags every week from open map data. This is a small browser game on my site, so treat the picture as a game's key art: one memorable moment, cinematic and grounded, painted rather than cartoonish, with no game interface, buttons or phone screens.

KEY VISUAL IDEAS:
- A cartographer's desk in a quiet map room at early morning: a large, beautifully aged world map spread across it, warm window light raking across the paper
- Dozens of small cloth flags on slim brass map pins planted all over the map, densest in Europe and the Americas and scattered across every continent, like a collection that has been growing for years; their colours make the map glow
- A hand (no face) about to press one more flag pin into the coast of the Pacific Northwest, where a few pins already stand
- A fine dashed line in ink running from one pin to another across an ocean, like a measured guess, and faint graticule lines and a compass rose on the map
- Behind the desk, a few more flags hanging softly from shelves and a window, the morning sun catching them: a new flag every day

DESIRED HOUSE STYLE:
Create a sophisticated editorial illustration that blends cinematic still-life photography, realistic textures of paper, brass, cloth and wood, map art and restrained data art. It should feel curious, warm and learned, like a geographer's study, not like an advertisement.

Use a panoramic 16:9 composition designed for a website hero and social sharing. Build the scene around the act of finding where a flag belongs rather than showing a screenshot. Integrate the map, the pins and the measuring line naturally into the scene. They should feel part of the world, not pasted on as a user interface.

Keep the composition visually rich but readable. Use strong depth, a clear visual path from the hand to the pin to the map, realistic detail, and one memorable idea. Leave some breathing room around the title.

TITLE:
Add only the exact title "FLAG ATLAS" in the upper-left. Use large, restrained uppercase lettering with generous tracking in a refined editorial serif. Add one short, thin accent rule beneath it.

COLOR AND MOOD:
Warm parchment, aged-map cream, brass and walnut, deep ink-navy shadows, with the flags as small bright points of red, blue, gold, green and white. The image should feel joyful, curious and exploratory, the pleasure of a world that keeps getting bigger the more you look.

IMPORTANT CONSTRAINTS:
- Spell the title exactly.
- Add no other words, labels, numbers, logos, or watermarks. No readable place names on the map.
- Keep the flags simple bold designs in the spirit of real flags (stripes, crosses, stars, suns, crescents, bands) but do not copy any specific real flag exactly, and draw no seals, coats of arms or lettering on them.
- Do not show a phone, app screen, dashboard or infographic.
- Show no faces; the person is only a hand.
- Avoid generic stock-photo composition and avoid cartoon styling.
