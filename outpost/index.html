OUTPOST

A homemade sensor station that reports home by radio.

secchi reads UNR's lake sensors and ALPINE-WATCH reads satellites. OUTPOST would be the first one that's mine end to end: a small box out in the world measuring water and weather, sending its readings home over the air, and landing on this site like the other dashboards. It starts on the kitchen table and works its way out, maybe down Chambers Creek to the Sound, maybe up to an alpine lake.

The candidate site

Chambers Creek, lake to Sound

Chambers Creek drains Lake Steilacoom, right here in Lakewood, and runs through a wooded canyon to Chambers Bay and Puget Sound. The USGS has gauged it below Leach Creek since the late 1930s, so a homemade station near the gauge could be checked against a professional one. Here's that gauge, live.

The plan

Four steps out the door

  1. 0

    Kitchen table

    One node, one temperature probe in a glass of water, talking LoRa to a Raspberry Pi in the house. The Pi commits a reading to a GitHub repo every 15 minutes, and a bare dashboard draws it. Proves the whole chain before anything gets wet.

    Weeks · no permits · no license needed

    See the shopping list →
  2. 1

    The backyard

    Same node in a weatherproof box on a fence post with a small solar panel. Run it through a Northwest winter, which is the real test of the power budget below, and learn how often it can sleep.

    A season · the battery's hardest months

  3. 2

    Chambers Creek

    Water temperature and level near the USGS gauge, with permission from whoever owns the bank. Sitting beside a federal gauge means every reading can be checked against a calibrated one, which is how a homemade station earns trust. The link home may be LoRa through a relay, or APRS once licensed.

    Needs landowner permission · ham license helps

  4. 3

    An alpine lake

    A summer-season station at a lake ALPINE-WATCH already watches from orbit, giving satellite water temperature its first ground truth. Too far for LoRa, so it either sends by satellite or logs to a card and reports when someone hikes in.

    Forest Service permit · satellite or store-and-forward

    Getting the data home

    Four ways to phone home

    Start here

    LoRa / Meshtastic

    License-free radio at 915 MHz, a few miles in the open with tiny power. Meshtastic firmware can already send sensor telemetry, and nodes relay for each other.

    Range
    1–10+ mi, line of sight
    Cost
    Cheap
    License
    None

    Where the ham license pays off

    APRS telemetry

    Weather and sensor packets on 144.390 MHz, picked up by the volunteer digipeater and internet gateway network and shown on aprs.fi. Rules apply: your call sign in every packet and nothing encrypted.

    Range
    Regional, via digipeaters
    Cost
    Moderate
    License
    Technician or up

    If there's a signal

    Cellular (LTE-M)

    A low-power cellular modem sends straight to the internet with no gateway. Simple, but it needs coverage and a monthly data plan.

    Range
    Anywhere with service
    Cost
    Monthly fee
    License
    None

    Satellite

    Short Iridium messages work from anywhere with sky. Each message costs money, so it sends a daily summary rather than every reading.

    Range
    Global
    Cost
    Per message
    License
    None

    Will it last the winter?

    Power budget

    The hard part of a remote station is keeping it alive through December in the Northwest. Play with the numbers. The defaults are a sleeping ESP32 with LoRa, four readings an hour, two 18650 cells and a small panel on a gray winter day.

    To think about

    Open questions

    What question does it answer?

    "How warm does the creek get in August?" or "how fast does it rise after a storm?" leads to different sensors, intervals and sites. The best stations start with one question.

    Whose bank is it?

    Much of lower Chambers Creek is Pierce County land, and the banks have owners. Ask before anything goes in the water, and make the box look like it belongs to someone who asked.

    Floods and curious hands

    Creeks rise and boxes walk off. Mount above the high-water line, anchor the probe in a perforated pipe, and put a friendly label with a contact on it.

    How it will show up

    Same pattern as secchi and ALPINE-WATCH: data in a repo, a GitHub Pages dashboard, maybe a nudge to the weather bots when the creek does something interesting.
