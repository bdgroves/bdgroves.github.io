📡 Ham License Cheat Sheet

A condensed study sheet for the Technician (Element 2) and General (Element 3) exams: the rules, the numbers and the formulas that show up again and again, plus the traps. Plan: pass both at the same sitting.

How to use this
Read a section, then drill those questions on HamStudy.org. Book the exam once you score 85% or better three times in a row on each element. Both exams draw one question from each of 35 topic groups, so no single topic can sink you. Press e to expand every section, c to collapse.

Series and parallel
Resistors and inductors add in series; in parallel use 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ (two equal ones = half). Capacitors are the opposite: they add in parallel and combine like resistors in series. Series: same current everywhere. Parallel: same voltage across each.

🌐 T3 · Radio waves and propagation
VHF / UHF

Mostly line of sight; the radio horizon is a bit farther than the visual one because the atmosphere bends signals. UHF gets into buildings better (shorter wavelength).

Polarization: vertical for FM and repeaters, horizontal for SSB/CW weak-signal work. Mismatched polarization costs a lot of signal.

The ionosphere

The D layer absorbs lower HF in daytime. The F layer (F2) reflects signals for long-distance contacts. Higher sunspots support higher frequencies.

The 2026 pool says signals "reflect" off the ionosphere.

Unusual VHF paths

Sporadic E: 6 m and 10 m openings, often in summer. Meteor scatter: best on 6 m. Tropospheric ducting: VHF/UHF hundreds of miles along weather fronts.

Aurora: signals sound raspy. Knife-edge diffraction: signals bend around a sharp ridge or building edge.

Fading

Picket fencing: rapid fluttering of a mobile FM signal from multipath. Moving the antenna a few feet can fix multipath dropouts.

Frequency × wavelength = speed of light: higher frequency, shorter wavelength.

Parts and what they do

Diode: current one way (rectifier) · LED: lights when forward-biased · Transistor: amplifier or switch · Relay: switch worked by an electromagnet · Fuse: opens on overload · Potentiometer: variable resistor (volume) · Transformer: changes AC voltage

Circuits

Conductors (copper) vs insulators (glass, plastic). A short blows the fuse; an open stops current. A tuned circuit (inductor + capacitor) picks one frequency.

Schematic symbols show up: learn resistor, capacitor, inductor, diode, transistor, battery, ground.

🗼 T9 · Antennas and feed lines
Antennas

A dipole radiates broadside to its wire. A quarter-wave vertical is omnidirectional. A Yagi beams in one direction for gain. A 5/8-wave vertical has more gain than a 1/4-wave.

The "rubber duck" on a handheld is short and inefficient; an outside antenna helps a lot. To make an antenna resonate lower, make it longer.

Feed lines

Coax is usually 50 Ω. Loss goes up with frequency and with length; air or foam dielectric loses less than solid. Water in the coax is the classic cause of rising loss.

Connectors: PL-259/SO-239 for HF, Type N for UHF and microwaves.

Quick test
High SWR means the antenna system isn't matched; the fix is at the antenna, though a tuner can make the radio happy.

☀️ G3 · Radio wave propagation 3 questions
The sun

Sunspot cycle ≈ 11 years. More sunspots → higher MUF → 10–15 m open up. The solar flux index (10.7 cm radio noise) tracks it; higher is better.

A flare's UV and X-rays arrive in about 8 minutes and can black out HF on the sunlit side. Charged particles from a CME arrive in 20–40 hours and bring geomagnetic storms and aurora.

The indices

K index: geomagnetic activity over 3 hours, 0–9. A index: the daily average. Low K and A = good HF conditions.

MUF: highest frequency that comes back to Earth on a path. LUF: lowest usable before absorption wins. Operate just under the MUF.

The layers

D layer: daytime absorption of 160–40 m, gone at night. E layer: single hop up to about 1,200 mi. F2 layer: highest, single hop up to about 2,500 mi.

Critical angle: launch any steeper and the signal goes into space.

Paths

NVIS: low antenna, high angle, 40/80 m, fills in 0–300 mi around you. Grey line: enhanced along the day/night boundary. Long path: the other way around the planet; the beam heading is 180° off.

Skip zone: too far for ground wave, too close for the first hop.

⚡ G5 · Electrical principles 3 questions
Reactance and impedance

Reactance is opposition to AC from L and C. Inductors resist more as frequency rises; capacitors resist less. Impedance = resistance + reactance; matching it transfers the most power.

At resonance, XL = XC.

Power and AC

RMS is the heating-equivalent DC voltage. PEP is the average power over one RF cycle at the highest peak of the envelope. For an unmodulated carrier, PEP = average.

A transformer's impedance ratio is the square of its turns ratio.

Common calculations
Three 100 Ω resistors in parallel → 33.3 Ω · Two 10 µF capacitors in series → 5 µF · 120 V RMS → 339 V peak-to-peak · A 2,250-turn primary on 120 V with 500 turns secondary → about 26.7 V

🗼 G9 · Antennas and feed lines 4 questions
Dipoles and verticals

Half-wave dipole ≈ 468 / f ft; feed-point impedance about 72 Ω in free space, changing with height. Quarter-wave ground-mounted vertical ≈ 36 Ω; sloping its radials down raises it toward 50 Ω.

A trap dipole works several bands. A loading coil makes a short antenna resonate. An end-fed half-wave has a very high feed impedance and needs a matching transformer.

Yagis

The reflector is longer than the driven element; directors are shorter. A longer boom with more directors means more gain and a narrower beam.

Front-to-back ratio: forward signal vs. the back. Gain in dBi = dBd + 2.15.

Feed lines

Ladder line (window line) has much less loss than coax, even at high SWR. Coax loss grows with frequency and length. High SWR adds loss in the line as heat.

An antenna tuner at the radio doesn't fix the SWR on the line; it only makes the radio see 50 Ω.

Patterns

A dipole's pattern is a figure eight, strongest broadside. A low horizontal antenna sends energy straight up (good for NVIS). Raising it lowers the radiation angle for DX.

A log-periodic covers a wide frequency range with fairly flat gain.
